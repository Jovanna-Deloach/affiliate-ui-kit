# Composing pages

A page is a sequence of **blocks** in the page well, separated by the theme’s section gap. The templates in `@/kit/templates` are the reference compositions: start from the one that fits, and when a page needs a different order, compose the blocks the same way. Blocks are sized by container queries, so each one fits the column it is put in.

## The archetypes

Every archetype takes the same `SiteChrome` and `RoundupGuide`, so content moves between them unchanged.

| Template | Use it for | Sections, in order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `RoundupPage` | “The best X”: three or more products ranked on the same measurements | Masthead · article header · quick verdict (up to three picks) · comparison, with what failed · a deep dive per pick · method · footer |
| `VersusPage` | “X vs. Y”: a decision between two products | Masthead · article header · two verdict cards side by side · a two-row comparison · both deep dives · method · footer |
| `LabAssayPage` | One product tested in depth, such as a lab assay | Masthead · article header · one verdict card across the width · the full review · method · footer |
| `CollectorsGuidePage` | A curated set of peers, not ranked | Masthead · article header · a catalog of every piece · each piece in full · method · footer |

The answer comes first (the quick verdict), the proof follows (comparison, deep dives), and the method closes. The verdict and the deep dive never repeat each other: the verdict card says why or who, the deep dive what was measured.

## The supplement pages

Each takes a `SiteChrome` and its own contract. The templates typeset the copy, set `data-category`, and apply the commerce rules for you: the disclosure notice before the first buy link, a standing warning in place of the sale, and the health disclaimer last.

| Template | Contract | Sections, in order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ProductPage` | `ProductReport` | Masthead · (a warning’s banner) · article header with the reviewer · (the full warning) · the review with its buy box · the lab report: grade, key findings, every result, the watchlist, the certification · the research on its main ingredient · price over 90 days and a price alert · readers’ reviews · people also looked at · questions · method · health disclaimer · footer, with a sticky buy bar |
| `BrandPage` | `BrandProfile` | Masthead · the brand: what it makes, products tested and certified, readers’ rating, how it handles reviews, the disclosure notice · (its warning) · its products · readers’ reviews · similar brands · health disclaimer · footer |
| `CategoryPage` | `CategoryGuide` | Masthead · article header · the ranking · the category report · questions beside their sources · method · Suggest a product · health disclaimer · footer |
| `IngredientPage` | `IngredientGuide` | Masthead · article header with the reviewer · beside a table of contents: what it is (other names, cited paragraphs) · the evidence · how to take it · safety · the studies · products tested · questions · sources · health disclaimer · footer |
| `SearchPage` | the query and its hits | Masthead · the query as the heading and a field to search again · results grouped by kind, or what to try and popular products · footer |
| `AccountPage` | the reader and their lists | Masthead with the account menu · tabs for Saved, Price alerts and History · the list · footer |

A `ProductReport` holds the review as a `Pick`, so a product reviewed in a guide gets its own page from the same data. Under an active warning, `ProductPage` leads with `Banner` and `ConsumerWarning`, and drops the grade, the certification, the price, every buy link and the structured data. It prints the readers’ rating as Product structured data only from the reviews it shows, and no other template prints review markup.

## The page skeleton

This is the roundup template, written out. Blocks marked “own well” render their own page well; wrap the others in `kit-well`.

```tsx
import {
  ComparisonMatrix,
  DisqualifiedSubTable,
  EditorialArticleHeader, // own well
  MethodologyAccordion, // own well
  ProductDeepDiveCard,
  QuickVerdictBento,
  SiteFooter, // own well
  SiteHeader, // own well
} from "@/kit/blocks";
import { typesetDeep } from "@/kit/lib/typeset";
import type { RoundupGuide, SiteChrome } from "@/kit/types";

export function Page({ site, guide: input }: { site: SiteChrome; guide: RoundupGuide }) {
  const guide = typesetDeep(input);
  const attribution = { source: "caliber", medium: "affiliate", campaign: guide.id };
  return (
    // data-theme sits on an ancestor (in the app, on <html>); data-category comes from the guide.
    <div data-category={guide.category} className="min-h-dvh bg-ground text-fg">
      <SiteHeader site={site} category={guide.category} />
      <main>
        <EditorialArticleHeader guide={guide} notice={{ text: site.disclosureNotice, href: site.disclosureHref }} />
        <div className="kit-well mt-12 md:mt-16">
          <QuickVerdictBento picks={guide.picks} jumpLabel={guide.jumpLabel} attribution={attribution} />
        </div>
        <div className="kit-well mt-chapter">
          <ComparisonMatrix heading={guide.compare.heading} intro={guide.compare.intro} metrics={guide.metrics} picks={guide.picks} attribution={attribution}>
            {guide.notRecommended && <DisqualifiedSubTable items={guide.notRecommended} metrics={guide.metrics} />}
          </ComparisonMatrix>
        </div>
        <div className="kit-well mt-chapter flex flex-col gap-10">
          {guide.picks.map((pick) => (
            <ProductDeepDiveCard key={pick.product.id} pick={pick} disclosureHref={site.disclosureHref} attribution={attribution} />
          ))}
        </div>
        <MethodologyAccordion methodology={guide.methodology} className="mt-chapter" />
      </main>
      <SiteFooter site={site} />
    </div>
  );
}
```

**Spacing between sections**: the first band after the article header takes `mt-12 md:mt-16`; every later section takes `mt-chapter`; stacked deep dives take `gap-10`. Inside blocks, spacing is the blocks’ own: do not add margins inside them.

**Do not** wrap a block in another card, put a label above a heading, add a background band behind a section, or put two blocks side by side that are designed to span the well (the article header, the verdict, the comparison, a deep dive, the method).

## Where each rule lives

| Rule | Where it is kept |
| --- | --- |
| The disclosure notice comes before the first buy link | `EditorialArticleHeader` sets the notice in the byline; keep the header first in `<main>` |
| “We may earn a commission.” under every buy box | `BuyBlock` adds it; never render an `OfferCard` without the buy box around it on a page |
| The full disclosure statement | `SiteFooter`, from `site.disclosure` |
| Lowest price first, sold out last | `BuyBlock`, `VerdictCard`, `ComparisonMatrix` and `PickRow` rank offers themselves; pass all of them |
| One filled button in view | The feature `VerdictCard` and the comparison’s first row carry it; every other card is outlined. Keep the lead pick first in `picks` |
| One buy box per product | Each `ProductDeepDiveCard` has one; do not add another for the same product |
| Jump links reach their review | A deep dive’s id is `review-{slug}` (`reviewId(pick)` from `@/kit/components`); verdict cards and catalog entries link to it |
| The method is linked from the testing card | `MethodologyAccordion`’s id defaults to `methodology`, the testing card’s default target |
| Every review stays up, whatever its rating | `ReviewsSection` shows every review, newest first, and says so under the list; only the reader filters it |
| A link to a review reaches it | A review’s id is `review-{id}` (its `Review.id`); `ReviewsSection` clears the filters that hide it and lengthens the list to show it |
| History stays with the reader | `@/kit/lib/reader` keeps viewed, saved and compared items on the device only; `PickUpWhereYouLeftOff` and `SavedItems` say so and keep Clear history in view |
| No buy link on a failure | `RankingList` and `ProductCard` leave out the buy pill for a product graded F; the ranking says why |
| A warning outranks the sale | Set `warning` on the product: `BuyBlock`, `VerdictCard`, `ComparisonMatrix`, `PickRow`, `ProductDeepDiveCard`, `ProductCard` and `RankingList` show `WarningNote` in place of its price, score and buy links, and drop its certification; `StickyBuyBar` stays hidden; `sortProducts` puts it last |
| Every claim is cited | `Citation` takes the source’s number (`n`) and `Reference.id` (`to`); `ReferenceList` takes the ids the text cites (`cited`) and links each back to its first citation; a source cited twice takes `occurrence={2}` |
| One FAQ carries structured data | `Faq` prints the FAQPage JSON-LD; on a page with a second `Faq`, pass `structuredData={false}` to it |
| The note reaches the full warning | `ConsumerWarning` takes `id={warningId(product)}` (`warning-{slug}`, from `@/kit/lib/warnings`): the notes in a guide link there; cards and ranking rows link to the product’s page |
| The ranking discloses before its buy pills | `RankingList` puts “We may earn a commission.” above the list, linked to `disclosureHref` |

## Reviews on a page

A product’s reviews go after the proof and before the method: `ReviewsSection` in a `kit-well` with `mt-chapter`, and `TrustPrinciples` under it when the page explains how reviews are handled. For reader reviews, pass `writeAction` (an outlined `Button` that opens a `Dialog` holding `WriteReview`) and the brand’s `TrustSignals` as `aside`. When the site’s testers write every review, leave both out: the section drops the filters that do not apply. A roundup does not list reviews of every pick; it links each pick to its own page.

Mark up reviews as structured data (schema.org `Product` with `Review` and `AggregateRating`) only on a single product’s page, and only for the reviews shown on it.

## Discovery and returning readers

- **The masthead** takes the site’s search and the reader’s menu in its slots: `<SiteHeader site={site} search={<SiteSearch index={hits} resultsPath="/search" />} account={<AccountMenu links={links} signInHref="/sign-in" />} />`. `QuickLinks` sits right under it, in its own well.
- **A category ranking page**: article header, then `RankingList` with `compare`, then the method; `CompareTray` once, last in `<main>`.
- **A product’s page** ends with `PeopleAlsoLookedAt` (its `products`); a brand’s page with its `brands`. Never on the same page as `CompareTray` and `StickyBuyBar` both: the bottom edge belongs to one of them.
- **Home and category pages** can show `PickUpWhereYouLeftOff` below the lead and `CategoryIndex` further down. The Saved page is `SavedItems`.
- **Remembering visits**: without an account, nothing reaches the device unless the page records it. Put `<RememberVisit item={toReaderItem(summary)} />` on every product page and `<RememberVisit item={{ kind: "guide", id, title, href }} />` on every guide, once (`RememberVisit` from `@/kit/lib/reader`, `toReaderItem` from `@/kit/lib/discovery`). With an account, pass the lists to the blocks as `items` with their callbacks instead.

## Warnings, safety and price alerts

- **A standing warning** about a product: set it on the product (`KitProduct.warning`), so every part that sells it shows the warning instead. On the product’s own page, put `Banner` with `tone="danger"` first under the masthead, linking to the full warning, and `ConsumerWarning` right under the article header, with `id={warningId(product)}`. Keep the product in its guides and rankings: it goes last, unlinked. Leave `CertificationNotice` off its page while the warning stands; the parts that take a product drop the certification on their own.
- **A resolved warning** stays on the product’s page as history: `ConsumerWarning` after the proof, in gray. The score and the buy links come back on their own.
- **A warning about a brand** (fake reviews, a regulator’s letter): `ConsumerWarning` with the brand as its `subject` at the top of the brand’s page, and the same warning on each product it covers, so their buy links come down too.
- **A certification that lapsed or is in testing** is not a warning: `CertificationNotice` goes where the page discusses the lab results, and the buy links stay.
- **Safety**: `SafetyPanel` on a supplement’s own page, after the proof and before the reviews. Keep its footer: it is general information, not medical advice.
- **Price history and alerts**: on a product’s page, `PriceHistory` after the buy box, then `PriceAlert` (or an outlined `Button` that opens it in a `Dialog`). Without an account, `PriceAlert` asks for an email; with one, pass `askEmail={false}`. The account’s Price alerts page is `AlertsManager`.

## Research and lab reports

- **A product’s lab report**: `GradeScale` beside its score in the header, then `KeyFindings`, `AssayTable` and `WatchlistIngredients` (its `id` is where `KeyFindings` links), then `CertificationNotice`. A grade’s meaning in full is `GradeLegend` with `LAB_GRADES`, in the method.
- **A supplement’s research page**: `AlsoKnownAs` under the title, then `EvidenceTable` (the answer), `DosageCard`, `SafetyPanel`, `ResearchFeed` (the proof), `Faq`, `ReferenceList`, and `HealthDisclaimer` last, before the footer. Every claim in the prose carries a `Citation`.
- **A category**: `RankingList`, then `CategoryReport`, with `SuggestAProduct` at the end (`CategoryPage` sets them out). When the ranking leaves products out, say so in its `summary`: “The 4 of 18 that passed every test.”
- **The byline**: give the guide `reviewedBy` (an `ExpertReview`) and `EditorialArticleHeader` shows `ReviewedBy` under the author. The reviewer’s `beats` must cover the page’s category.
- **Every supplement page** ends with `HealthDisclaimer`, visible, before `SiteFooter`; `plural` on a guide to several products.

## Composing beyond the templates

Blocks are container-driven, so the same blocks recompose into new archetypes: a long guide can follow its verdict with `AlsoGreatRail` for the remaining picks; a page with no ranking can drop the comparison; a lab page can show `DisqualifiedSubTable` with `standalone` when there is no comparison. Keep the order answer, proof, method; keep the header first and the footer last.

Helpers for custom compositions:

- `typesetDeep(guide)` from `@/kit/lib/typeset`: typeset every string in a guide (idempotent).
- `rankOffers(offers)` and `leadOffer(offers)` from `@/kit/lib/offers`: the kit’s merchant order, and the offer a product leads with.
- `pickName(pick)` and `reviewId(pick)` from `@/kit/components`: “Brand Model”, and the deep dive’s id.
- `filterReviews`, `summarize` and `ALL_REVIEWS` from `@/kit/lib/reviews`: a review list’s query, order and numbers.
- `gradeFor(score)`, `sortProducts(products, by)`, `toReaderItem(summary)` and `searchIndex(hits, query)` from `@/kit/lib/discovery`: a grade from a score out of 10, a ranking’s four orders, a product as the reader’s lists keep it, and search over a small index.
- `RememberVisit`, `useReaderList(list)`, `remember`, `forget` and `clearList` from `@/kit/lib/reader`: the reader’s device memory (client only).
- `underWarning(product)`, `warningId(product)` and `WARNING_LABEL` from `@/kit/lib/warnings`: whether a warning stands, the full warning’s id, and each kind’s label.
- `EVIDENCE_GRADES`, `LAB_GRADES`, `sortOutcomes`, `percentOff`, `passesPurity`, `summarizeResults`, `referenceId` and `citationId` from `@/kit/lib/evidence`: what grades mean, an evidence table’s orders, a lab report’s numbers, a category’s results, and the anchors citations jump between.

## In the app

Kit components are React Server Components; the buy links are the only client leaves (they track clicks). In a Next.js page, set the theme on `<html data-theme="…">`, load the theme’s three faces with `next/font`, and render the template or your composition from a Server Component.

# Making pages distinct

A watch magazine and a supplement lab must not look like one template recolored, and no two guides on one site should read as the same page with new nouns. The kit gets there with a small number of strong levers, applied together. It does not get there with per-page styling: every page meets the same quality floor because the look lives in the theme, not in the page.

## The levers, strongest first

1. **The theme is the publication.** `atelier` and `assay` differ in every role: faces, scale, palette, radii, elevation and density, not only color. A new publication gets a new theme: a new `[data-theme]` block in `src/kit/theme/kit.css` that sets every role in Foundations, with its own display face, its own type scale and its own surfaces. The surfaces stay cool whites and grays, never beige, cream or parchment: `bun run check` fails on a warm one. Never a new component variant.
2. **The category is the section.** Within one publication, `data-category` changes the wayfinding accent (the section marker, award text in tables, telemetry, stamps) and nothing else.
3. **The archetype is the argument.** A ranking (`RoundupPage`), a decision between two (`VersusPage`), one product in depth (`LabAssayPage`) or a catalog of peers (`CollectorsGuidePage`) are different pages, not one page with a different count.
4. **The sections are the evidence.** What a page proves decides what it shows: a comparison only when picks share measurements; what failed (`DisqualifiedSubTable`) when the testing excluded products; a catalog rail when the picks after the verdict are worth a second look; the method always.
5. **The measurements are the product category.** `Telemetry` sets a watch’s timing, a supplement’s heavy metals or a shoe’s bench results under the score; the assay stamp appears only on lab-tested products.
6. **The content is the page.** Headings that say something specific, a verdict, a “best for” and a synthesis that each say something different, real measurements, a named tester’s quote with its proof, and a photograph of every product. Two pages with the same blocks read differently when their words do.

## Variants that exist

Use these, and only these, to vary a part: they are documented on each component’s page.

- `VerdictCard`: `feature` or `compact`; `wide` for a single-pick page; `stacked` beside a feature card. `QuickVerdictBento` picks them from the number of picks.
- `SpecSheet`: `drawer` (a card footer) or `sheet` (always open).
- `Badge`: `outline`, `solid`, `frosted`, `soft` in a tone. `Award`, `BestPrice` and `ResultMark` are badges with their wording decided.
- `Price`: four sizes, a struck list price, a caption, sold out.
- `Meter`: continuous, with a reference tick, or in segments, in five tones.
- `Eyebrow`: three tones, two sizes, as a heading, in parts, with an icon.
- `ProductImage` and `PickMedia`: the ratio and padding for each place.

## Never

- Recolor, resize or restyle a component with `className`: it places a part (margin, grid position, width), it does not change its look.
- Add a color, a font size, a shadow or a radius that is not a role.
- Box everything: containers nest twice at most, and inside a deep dive the only box is the offer card.
- Rank with numbers (“01 — Best overall”), or give the filled action to more than one thing in view.
- Use stock lifestyle photography, a drawing or a chart in place of a product, or the same photograph for two products.
- Write generic headings (“Our top picks”, “Comparison”, “Methodology”), copy typed in capitals, emoji, or a sentence that appears twice.
- Hand-build a part the kit does not document. Say the page needs it instead.
